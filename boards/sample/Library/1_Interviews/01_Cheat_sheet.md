# Cheat sheet (start here)

Read this in the five minutes before a call. This sample also shows the markup every guide can use. Open it in a text editor next to the app to see how each piece is written.

## How guides work

- `## A heading` starts a card. `### A heading` starts a question inside the card. The page builds the table of contents and the jump keys from them.
- Lines that start with `>` are what you'd say out loud. Each one gets a **Copy answer** button.
- *<An example answer in angle brackets and stars, like this one.>* Examples show in a different color and are left out when you copy a card.
- ✎ after a question means the answer isn't confirmed yet. The Library adds a **My version** box under it; what you type is saved to `Library/My_answers.md`.
- ☐ marks a fact you still need to find out.
- `::: fold Title` … `:::` makes a block you can open and close. `::: fold + Title` starts open.
- `{.pay}` at the end of a heading marks a pay section. If you turn on `blurPay` in the settings, it stays blurred until you press H (handy when sharing your screen).

::: fold Show me a folded block
Folded blocks keep long material out of the way: a full story, a table, a posting.
:::

## Your opener

1. What you build, in one line: the kind of systems and the stack.
2. One result with a number: *<cut checkout API p99 latency from 900 ms to 250 ms>*.
3. What you want next, and why this team fits.
4. A question back to them.
5. Stop there. Under a minute.

> I'm a backend engineer who likes owning a service end to end, from the design doc to the pager. Most recently I rebuilt our checkout API and cut its p99 latency by more than two thirds. I'm looking for a team where reliability is part of the product, and this role reads that way.

## Always asked

### Why are you looking? ✎

> I've shipped the big project I joined for, and I want a bigger system and more ownership of its design.

- *<I want to work closer to payments and money movement.>*
- *<My team is moving to maintenance mode and I want to keep building.>*

### What's your biggest weakness? ✎

> I used to go quiet when I was stuck on a hard bug. Now I timebox it: after an hour I write down what I've tried and ask for a second pair of eyes, which usually saves the day.

- *<Over-polishing code before the first review; I now open draft pull requests early.>*
- ☐ Pick a real example from the last two years.

### Tell me about a failure.

> I shipped a schema migration that locked a busy table for four minutes. Now every migration I write runs against a production-sized copy first, and I added that check to our pipeline.

## Pay {.pay}

- Your target range: ☐ look up the level on public salary sites before the recruiter call.
- Your floor: keep it to yourself.
- If they ask first: *<I'd like to learn more about the level first. What range did you have in mind?>*

## Working with recruiters and agencies

- Register with each agency once, then get to know one recruiter there who places engineers like you.
- Only one agency should submit you for a given role. Log it on the recruiter's card under "Roles they submitted me for", and the Jobs tab will warn you before you apply there yourself.
- Check in every few weeks with something useful: a new project, the stack you want next.

## Never say

- Anything negative about your current team or company.
- A number before you know the level.
